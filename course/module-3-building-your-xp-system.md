# Module 3: Building Your XP System

> **Duration:** 20-25 minutes  
> **Goal:** Set up CommunityXP and implement engagement mechanics in your community

## Lesson 3.1: Installing CommunityXP

Let's get practical. It's time to build your engagement system.

### Step 1: Access the Whop App Store

1. Log in to your Whop dashboard
2. Navigate to your company's apps section
3. Search for "CommunityXP" in the app store
4. Click "Install"

### Step 2: Connect to Your Community

After installation, CommunityXP will:
- Automatically detect your existing members
- Begin tracking activity across your Whop apps
- Set up default XP rules

**Note:** Members who were already in your community won't lose anything—they'll start at Level 1 with 0 XP, and can begin earning immediately.

### Step 3: Configure Basic Settings

When you first open CommunityXP, you'll see your dashboard with:
- **Member Overview** — Total members and activity summary
- **Leaderboard** — Top contributors this period
- **Level Distribution** — How many members at each level

Take a moment to explore before we configure.

## Lesson 3.2: Setting Up XP Rules

XP rules define *what actions earn points*. This is where you translate your community goals into measurable behavior.

### Default XP Rules (Free Starter)

CommunityXP comes with sensible defaults:

| Action | XP Earned |
|--------|-----------|
| Sending a chat message | 1 XP |
| Creating a forum post | 5 XP |
| Receiving a reaction | 2 XP |
| Completing a course lesson | 10 XP |

These work for most communities. But you may want to adjust based on what you value.

### Customizing XP Values

Think about what behaviors matter most in *your* community:

| Community Type | High-Value Actions | XP Suggestion |
|----------------|-------------------|---------------|
| Course-based | Completing lessons, passing quizzes | 15-25 XP |
| Discussion-focused | Quality posts, helpful answers | 10-15 XP |
| Event-driven | Attending live events, participating | 20-50 XP |
| Networking | Introducing yourself, connecting with others | 10-20 XP |

### A Warning: Don't Reward Spam

If "sending a message" earns the most XP, you'll get lots of messages. But not necessarily good ones.

**Better approach:**
- Low XP for basic actions (messages, reactions)
- Higher XP for quality signals (receiving reactions, completing content)
- Bonus XP for meaningful milestones (first post, attending events)

Quality over quantity.

## Lesson 3.3: Designing Your Level Tiers

Levels create the sense of progression we discussed in Module 2. CommunityXP uses XP thresholds to define levels.

### Default Level Structure

| Level | XP Required | Name (Customizable) |
|-------|-------------|---------------------|
| 1 | 0 | Newcomer |
| 2 | 100 | Explorer |
| 3 | 300 | Contributor |
| 4 | 600 | Regular |
| 5 | 1,000 | Veteran |
| 6 | 1,500 | Expert |
| 7 | 2,500 | Master |
| 8 | 4,000 | Champion |
| 9 | 6,000 | Legend |
| 10 | 10,000 | Elite |

### Designing Good Level Curves

Notice the pattern: each level requires *more* XP than the last. This is called a **progressive curve**.

**Why it works:**
- Early levels feel achievable (motivation boost)
- Later levels feel earned (status value)
- The gap increases so high-level status stays rare

**Common mistake:** Linear scaling (100, 200, 300...) makes early levels too slow and removes later-level prestige.

### Naming Your Levels

Generic names like "Level 1, Level 2" work, but custom names create community identity.

Examples by community type:

| Type | Sample Level Names |
|------|-------------------|
| Trading | Scout → Analyst → Trader → Strategist → Master Trader |
| Fitness | Newbie → Starter → Regular → Athlete → Elite → Champion |
| Creative | Observer → Sketcher → Creator → Artist → Master → Virtuoso |
| Business | Member → Builder → Operator → Executive → Founder |

Choose names that resonate with your specific community.

## Lesson 3.4: Leaderboard Strategy

Leaderboards tap into competitive psychology. But they need careful design to help rather than hurt your community.

### Leaderboard Types in CommunityXP

**All-Time Leaderboard:**
- Shows members with the most total XP *ever*
- Good for: Recognizing true veterans
- Risk: Discouraging for new members

**Weekly/Monthly Leaderboard:**
- Resets periodically
- Good for: Keeping competition fresh
- Risk: Less recognition for consistency

**Recommended:** Use *both*. Weekly leaderboards for ongoing competition, all-time for legacy status.

### Making Leaderboards Healthy

**Do this:**
- Celebrate top performers publicly (weekly shoutouts)
- Show position changes ("You moved up 5 spots!")
- Have multiple categories (most helpful, most active, etc.)

**Avoid this:**
- Only recognizing #1 (everyone else feels ignored)
- Punishing low rankings
- Making leaderboard the *only* recognition system

### The "Middle Majority" Solution

Remember: most members will never hit the top 10. They need wins too.

**Solutions:**
- "Personal best" tracking — "You earned more XP this week than last!"
- Percentile recognition — "You're in the top 25% of members"
- Improvement badges — "Rising Star: Most improved this week"

Everyone should feel progress, not just the power users.

## Lesson 3.5: Badge Design

Badges are visible achievements that members can earn and display. They're powerful for recognition and social proof.

### The 8 Pre-Built Badges (Free Starter)

CommunityXP Free Starter includes 8 badges designed for universal use:

| Badge | Trigger |
|-------|---------|
| 🌱 First Steps | Member joins the community |
| 💬 Conversation Starter | First chat message sent |
| 📝 Author | First forum post created |
| ⭐ Popular | Received 10 reactions on posts |
| 🔥 On Fire | 7-day activity streak |
| 📚 Scholar | Completed first course lesson |
| 🤝 Networker | Reacted to 25 different members' posts |
| 🏆 Monthly MVP | #1 on monthly leaderboard |

These cover key behaviors and can be used immediately.

### Using Badges Effectively

**Make them visible:**
- Encourage members to display badges on profiles
- Reference badges in community announcements
- Celebrate when members earn key badges

**Make them meaningful:**
- Don't give badges for trivial actions
- Create "hard" badges that require real effort
- Use badge tiers (Bronze / Silver / Gold versions)

### Beyond Pre-Built Badges

The 8 pre-built badges cover common milestones. For behavior specific to your community, custom badges become valuable.

Examples of custom badges:
- "Case Study Hero" — Featured in a community case study
- "Event Regular" — Attended 5+ live events
- "Mentor" — Helped 10 new members
- "OG" — Member since launch month

> 💡 **Upgrade Path:** Custom badges tailored to your community's unique achievements are available with CommunityXP Pro. Start with the 8 pre-built badges to validate the system, then expand.
